How much does the action stream attend to video?

Updated 2026-10-29 19:58 · 5000 windows per model (Aloha-Agilex randomized_500 (100 random training windows per task), 2 episodes × 3 windows per task), 15000 forwards; video sigma 0.50; action sigmas 0.25, 0.50, 0.75; token grid (3, 12, 10) (frames × rows × cols), head rows 8

Caveat for this embodiment. The Aloha-Agilex clean_50 episodes are the checkpoints' own training data (dataloader variant "both" = clean_50 + randomized_500), so with video present the flow-matching error is near zero (the actions are memorised) and the error ratio is not meaningful here; read the absolute NMSE without video, the velocity change and the attention weights instead, and compare them with the Franka page, where the same quantities are out of distribution.

Training-faithful forwards on RoboTwin Aloha-Agilex randomized_500 (100 random training windows per task) clips (33-step window, 9 video frames at stride 4 → latent frames, 32 action steps, proprio token) through each checkpoint, with the attention of the action stream instrumented. The question: in the joint self-attention family, how much of the action tokens' attention goes to video, and how does the resulting video influence compare with the bridge cross-attention family?

The difference

Each row is one checkpoint; every number is a mean over the 32 action tokens, all heads, all 30 layers, all windows and both action noise levels. Attention weight columns read the softmax of the joint self-attention directly: what fraction of an action token's attention lands on video tokens versus on the other action tokens (they sum to 1; the proprio/text token lives in a separate cross-attention and is not part of this split). The cross-attention family runs two separate softmaxes (self-attention over action tokens, bridge attention over video tokens), each summing to 1, so the weight columns do not exist for it; the norm share columns put both families on the same footing: of the residual update the action stream receives from attention in a layer, the part that came from video versus from action tokens (self-attention: video-key part of the mixed attention output after o-projection and gate; cross-attention: bridge update versus self-attention update). The last two columns are causal: the same sample re-run with the action stream cut off from video.

Definitions

  • Attention weight on video / action (self-attention family): Σ of the softmax weights an action query places on video keys / on action keys.
  • Video share of the attention update: self-attention ‖gate · o(attention output restricted to video keys)‖ / ‖gate · o(full attention output)‖; cross-attention ‖gate_ca · bridge‖ / (‖gate_ca · bridge‖ + ‖gate_sa · self-attention‖). The action share is one minus this.
  • Velocity change when video is cut: ‖v_ablated − v‖ / ‖v‖ of the predicted flow velocity when action rows cannot attend any video key (self-attention) or the bridge term is dropped (cross-attention).
  • Flow-matching error ratio: NMSE against the training target without video ÷ with video; 1.0 would mean the video stream contributes nothing to the action prediction at these noise levels.
  • Per-layer values, the split by latent frame and camera region, per-head values and entropies are below.

Ablation decomposition (why does cutting video change the prediction but not always the error?)

Write the error with video as e = v − target and the change caused by cutting video as d = v_ablated − v. Then NMSE_without / NMSE_with = 1 + 2⟨e,d⟩/‖e‖² + ‖d‖²/‖e‖². The two terms separate two situations: ⟨e,d⟩ ≈ 0 means the video-driven part of the prediction is orthogonal to the residual error (video changes the prediction along directions the target does not reward or penalise); a negative ⟨e,d⟩ means cutting video partly cancels the error. The absolute NMSE with video is listed so a high baseline is visible. The velocity change is given three ways: norms summed over the 32 action tokens before dividing, the mean of per-token ratios, and the median of per-token ratios, next to the window-level ratio used in the headline table. Everything is split by action noise level.
