Downstream probes on OpenWAM representations

Seven OpenWAM architectures differ only in how the action branch is wired to the video branch. We ask whether that wiring changes what the video branch encodes, by probing every layer of every architecture on three downstream tasks, with VideoMAEv2, DINOv2 and D4RT probed identically as yardsticks. Every probe reads the full, unpooled token grid of one layer and is trained on 5 episodes per task.

How to read the scale

Absolute values are low on every task for every model, including the specialist encoders: this protocol uses 5 training episodes per task and coarse token grids (OpenWAM 8 × 10 per frame), so it measures what a tiny probe can read out, not the ceiling of the task. Findings

  1. OpenWAM carries more geometric and object information than the specialist encoders under this probe. On depth and segmentation every one of the 7 architectures beats VideoMAEv2, DINOv2 and D4RT at their best layers, on both robots (Agilex depth AbsRel 0.045 vs 0.053 for the best reference; segmentation IoU 0.18 vs 0.16). On task classification OpenWAM matches VideoMAEv2 on Franka and trails it by 2 points on Agilex.
  2. Measured against the reference span, the architecture spread is 1–12% of it at the best layer and 2–25% on the layer mean. The largest relative spreads are on classification (Franka layer mean 25%, Agilex best layer 11%) and segmentation (Agilex best layer 12%); depth pairwise accuracy separates architectures least (1–6%) because every model is above 0.9 on it.
  3. The ordering of architectures is not stable. The architecture that leads a task on Agilex does not lead it on Franka (classification: idm vs video_sees_action; depth: isolated vs idm; segmentation: cross_attention vs video_generation), and no architecture leads at a majority of layers on any task.
  4. Policy success does not follow the visual probes. The 92% tier and the 88% tier differ by under 0.3% on every Σ30, and Spearman correlations between Σ30 and success are weak and change sign between robots. The wiring that moves success (action attending to video, or detaching the action gradient as in video_generation) leaves the video branch's depth, segmentation and semantic read-outs where they were.
  5. Specialist encoders do not close the gap either. Probed identically, Depth Anything 3's backbone reaches AbsRel 0.053 / 0.097 (Agilex / Franka) and SAM ViT-H's encoder IoU 0.110 / 0.077, against 0.044 / 0.085 and 0.183 / 0.119 for the best OpenWAM architecture, so every OpenWAM architecture beats the depth specialist on depth and the segmentation specialist on segmentation under this probe. The zero-shot ceilings separate the two tasks: DA3's own relative-depth prediction (scale + shift aligned) scores 0.063 / 0.103, still behind a 5-episode probe on OpenWAM tokens, whereas SAM prompted with the GT box + centroid reaches IoU 0.82 / 0.85. Depth read-out is therefore near what a dedicated model delivers; segmentation IoU is capped by the token-level probe protocol (8 × 10 to 16 × 21 tokens upsampled to 120 × 160), not by what the encoders know about the object.
  6. Motion read-out (3D scene flow, 2D tracking) follows the same pattern. All 7 architectures land within 0.4 mm of each other at the best layer (Agilex 15.7–16.1 mm, Franka 22.6–23.0 mm; Σ30 range under 1%), between the specialist D4RT encoder (14.9 / 19.6 mm) and VideoMAEv2 (20.6 / 29.7 mm). TAPVid AJ tells the same story (Agilex .441–.446, Franka .400–.407 vs D4RT .448 / .429 and VideoMAEv2 .378 / .341). video_generation and idm, the two architectures where the video branch receives no action gradient or the most direct one, bracket the ranking on Agilex and swap places on Franka, so the action wiring leaves motion information untouched as well. Flow peaks at layers 10–12.
  7. OpenWAM peaks early. Classification peaks at layers 7–9 of 30, depth and segmentation at layers 12–15, and every layer after 1 stays within a narrow band; VideoMAEv2 and DINOv2 rise steadily to layers 13–22 and VideoMAEv2 drops 8–11 points in its last two layers.

Metrics

Mean |predicted − true| / true depth per pixel. 0.05 means 5% error on average. The reference encoders span 0.053–0.079 (Agilex) and 0.091–0.139 (Franka).
δ1 ↑
Share of pixels within a factor 1.25 of the true depth.
Pairwise ↑
DA-2K-style relative depth: of 200 random pixel pairs per frame whose true depths differ by ≥ 10%, the share ordered correctly. 0.5 is chance; every model scores above 0.9, so this metric saturates and separates models least.

Metrics

EPE3D ↓
3D endpoint error in mm: for a query pixel at probe frame k, the distance between the predicted and true 3D displacement of that point to frame k+1 (16 raw frames ≈ 0.5 s). Ground truth comes from the per-entity poses recorded in the replay (_extra.hdf5), so it is exact for every pixel on a known entity. Queries are stratified into robot links, moving objects (|Δ| > 1 mm) and static scene, 16 each per step.
zero-motion, rel
EPE of predicting "nothing moved", and EPE3D divided by it. rel < 1 means the probe reads real motion out of the tokens; the static group is where zero-motion is already perfect.
Probe
Attention read-out whose query encodes (u, v); it attends over the tokens of frames k and k+1 (frame-tagged) and regresses Δxyz plus a visibility logit. 60 epochs on 5 episodes per task; steps k = 1..7 for every model.

Caveats

Limits of this evidence

  • One probe seed, one train/test split. Architecture gaps of the size observed cannot be ranked until bootstrap intervals over test episodes are added.
  • Success rates are single evaluations from reports/performance.md; their own uncertainty is unknown.
  • Probe budget. Classification uses 10 epochs (about 5 points short of convergence); depth and segmentation use 60 because their linear heads were far from converged at 10. The same budget applies to every model and layer.
  • Tasks 4–5 use the per-entity poses added to the dataset on 2026-10-05; the probe's own metrics are listed in each section.
