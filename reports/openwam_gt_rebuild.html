Downstream probes on OpenWAM representations

Seven OpenWAM architectures differ only in how the action branch is wired to the video branch. We ask whether that wiring changes what the video branch encodes, by probing every layer of every architecture on three downstream tasks, with VideoMAEv2, DINOv2 and D4RT probed identically as yardsticks. Every probe reads the full, unpooled token grid of one layer and is trained on 5 episodes per task.

How to read the scale

Absolute values are low on every task for every model, including the specialist encoders: this protocol uses 5 training episodes per task and coarse token grids (OpenWAM 8 × 10 per frame), so it measures what a tiny probe can read out, not the ceiling of the task. The meaningful scale is therefore the span the three reference encoders occupy. The tiles below express the spread across the 7 OpenWAM architectures as a fraction of that span.

Findings

  1. OpenWAM carries more geometric and object information than the specialist encoders under this probe. On depth and segmentation every one of the 7 architectures beats VideoMAEv2, DINOv2 and D4RT at their best layers, on both robots (Agilex depth AbsRel 0.045 vs 0.053 for the best reference; segmentation IoU 0.18 vs 0.16). On task classification OpenWAM matches VideoMAEv2 on Franka and trails it by 2 points on Agilex.
  2. Measured against the reference span, the architecture spread is 1–12% of it at the best layer and 2–25% on the layer mean. The largest relative spreads are on classification (Franka layer mean 25%, Agilex best layer 11%) and segmentation (Agilex best layer 12%); depth pairwise accuracy separates architectures least (1–6%) because every model is above 0.9 on it.
  3. The ordering of architectures is not stable. The architecture that leads a task on Agilex does not lead it on Franka (classification: idm vs video_sees_action; depth: isolated vs idm; segmentation: cross_attention vs detach), and no architecture leads at a majority of layers on any task.
  4. Policy success does not follow the visual probes. The 92% tier and the 88% tier differ by under 0.3% on every Σ30, and Spearman correlations between Σ30 and success are weak and change sign between robots. The wiring that moves success (action attending to video, detaching the cross-attention gradient) leaves the video branch's depth, segmentation and semantic read-outs where they were.
  5. OpenWAM peaks early. Classification peaks at layers 7–9 of 30, depth and segmentation at layers 12–15, and every layer after 1 stays within a narrow band; VideoMAEv2 and DINOv2 rise steadily to layers 13–22 and VideoMAEv2 drops 8–11 points in its last two layers.

δ1 ↑
Share of pixels within a factor 1.25 of the true depth.
Pairwise ↑

Caveats

Limits of this evidence

  • One probe seed, one train/test split. Architecture gaps of the size observed cannot be ranked until bootstrap intervals over test episodes are added.
  • Success rates are single evaluations from reports/performance.md; their own uncertainty is unknown.
  • Probe budget. Classification uses 10 epochs (about 5 points short of convergence); depth and segmentation use 60 because their linear heads were far from converged at 10. The same budget applies to every model and layer.
  • Not covered. Action-relevant information (predicting joint motion from video tokens) is the dimension these architectures were designed to differ on and has not been probed yet; 3D flow and tracking lack GT.
